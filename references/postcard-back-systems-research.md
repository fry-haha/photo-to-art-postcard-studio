# Postcard back systems: researched premium reserve

Use this reference when the user asks for a designed postcard back rather than a generic postal template. The reserve was distilled from museum, artist-edition, exhibition, modern, and vintage postcard examples found through visual web research. Select by subject and front-side art direction; do not apply all motifs at once.

These systems specify spatial structure, not a mandatory ivory palette. Before selecting a system, choose a source-derived hero ground with `visual-system.md`. Coastal water may produce light blue; an art-space floor may produce brown with white postal lines; warm music light may produce yellow/amber; a lake may produce blue-white; architecture may use façade/stone color; and a portrait may use a distinctive clothing color. Preserve a neutral ground only when it has a deliberate front/back reason.

## Ten systems

1. **Soft wash corner** — a meaningful photo fragment dissolves from one corner into paper. Use for coast, lake, forest, or contextual portrait. The fade must use a broad irregular alpha field and paper grain; no visible rectangle, oval, or single linear gradient.
2. **Panoramic footer** — a shallow, complete horizon or landmark silhouette runs along the lower edge while the upper field stays writable. Use when lateral continuity matters.
3. **Blind watermark** — an extremely pale complete subject silhouette or contour sits behind the postal structure like an emboss. Use for restrained museum or portrait backs.
4. **Architectural blueprint** — a complete elevation, ceiling bay, column rhythm, or structural trace occupies one outer zone. Use only when geometry is legible and meaningful.
5. **Archive contact sheet** — one or two small, consistently sized image cells with accession-like metadata. Use for travel archives and art spaces; never repeat the same full image in decorative frames.
6. **Field-note margin** — a narrow handwritten or typed observation margin balances a large open writing field. Use for Artist Travel Archive.
7. **Museum accession label** — a compact curatorial label, catalog number placeholder, and precise hairlines on clean cotton-rag paper. Use for Museum Art Collection.
8. **Edge index** — place, time, collection, or edition runs along one edge; the rest stays open. Use for minimal contemporary cards.
9. **Circular groove** — one or two fine concentric arcs or a cropped record detail organize the stamp/address relationship. Use for vinyl and music objects; keep it quiet.
10. **Subject material band** — a narrow strip derived from water, stone, textile, vegetation, or record surface anchors an edge. Use when the material is characteristic of the subject.

## Pairing rules

- Landscape: soft wash corner, panoramic footer, field-note margin, blind watermark.
- Architecture/art space: architectural blueprint, archive contact sheet, museum accession label.
- Music/vinyl: circular groove, edge index, archive contact sheet.
- Portrait: soft wash corner, subject material band, blind watermark.
- D1 should normally retain a photographic echo; D2 should use archive/field-study logic; D3 should be the quietest and most exact. Quiet does not mean colorless.
- Every back keeps a clear stamp position, address treatment, writing area, and the same orientation/dimensions as the front.

## Transition quality

Blend photographic echoes with at least three interacting signals: a broad directional falloff, a softly blurred irregular/noise field, and local paper-grain modulation. When converting photo to sketch, let architectural or subject contours continue across the boundary. A single ellipse, rectangle, feathered box, or uniform Gaussian blur is a failure.
