# Text and layout library

Text is optional. Select one direction per postcard and keep the set to no more than two type personalities. Use exact supplied text. Use `PLACE` and `TIME` only as visible template placeholders.

## 36 directions

1. Museum label — small serif caption at lower left.
2. Archive accession — monospace ID, date, and place in a narrow strip.
3. Contact-sheet index — frame number, roll number, and tiny technical line.
4. Film-edge code — compact text aligned vertically along one edge.
5. Gallery title — wide tracking, centered, ample empty space.
6. Editorial masthead — strong sans title with a small serif deck.
7. Newspaper column — narrow caption column beside the photograph.
8. Photo-credit line — discreet credit and location under a full-bleed image.
9. Typewriter note — left-aligned personal note with uneven line rhythm.
10. Postmark cluster — circular stamp, date, and place grouped in one corner.
11. Air-mail route — origin and destination connected by a thin rule.
12. Boarding-pass band — place, time, sequence, and code in a bottom band.
13. Ticket stub — perforation cue, serial number, and compact fields.
14. Field-note margin — handwritten annotation in the outer margin.
15. Diary date — oversized date with a tiny place caption.
16. Coordinates line — coordinates or `PLACE` with a fine hairline.
17. Map legend — tiny keys and symbols aligned in a grid.
18. Specimen label — subject name, collection, and number.
19. Split bilingual — two languages mirrored across a center line.
20. Vertical title — vertical display word with horizontal metadata.
21. Corner whisper — very small type confined to one corner.
22. Bottom ticker — a continuous caption line along the lower edge.
23. Top utility bar — place, time, and project name in a thin top bar.
24. Side index — numbered information running down one side.
25. Centered dedication — one restrained sentence in the negative space.
26. Lyric fragment — exact supplied lyric set as a short poetic block.
27. Zine cut-label — short words in separate rectangular labels.
28. Photocopy headline — condensed uppercase title with rough secondary copy.
29. Minimal Swiss grid — strict left alignment and modular metadata.
30. Fashion editorial — tall display type with tiny credits.
31. Concert setlist — exact supplied lines in a compact monospaced list.
32. Caption brackets — title or place enclosed by small bracket marks.
33. Stamp-frame title — text held inside a perforated stamp-like frame.
34. Image-overprint — one large translucent word over negative space, never over a face.
35. Blank correspondence — no front text; only address lines and stamp box on back.
36. Modular placeholders — `PLACE`, `TIME`, and `NOTE` fields designed as replaceable components.

## Selection rules

- Match the image, not a fixed template.
- Keep faces, focal objects, and readable signs unobstructed.
- Use no text when the photograph is stronger without it.
- Placeholders must look intentional, not like unfilled form errors.
- Do not render long or small critical copy directly through image generation when deterministic typesetting is available.

## Postcard back systems: 50-layout reserve

The back must feel designed with the front, not attached from a generic postal template. A center divider is optional. Preserve a usable address area, stamp area, safe area, and writing space when the card is intended to be mailed.

### Locked signature traits from approved tests

When an earlier back has been approved, treat its organizing idea as locked reference material. Refinement may improve spacing or legibility, but must not simplify away its identity. The approved reserve includes:

- an open film-white or film-black field with a large, faint subject silhouette or line-art echo;
- a small arch, sail, window, or film-cell photo echo used as the stamp area;
- visible `PLACE` and `TIME` fields when real copy has not been supplied;
- a full-bleed, low-contrast photograph with a pale floating correspondence panel;
- a subject-derived material band, such as textile, masonry, vegetation, water, or record surface;
- an offset/open address cluster rather than a compulsory center divider;
- when an approved example used tape, preserve its attachment or connection task without copying the exact strip; tape remains optional in other layouts.

Do not replace these with a generic thumbnail, literal stamp rectangle, and four identical lines. Select the back motif from the subject: landmark silhouette for architecture, landscape trace or full-art field for nature, textile/material band for portrait, and record/groove or equipment detail for music.

### Warm three-column comparison system

When the user selects the warm museum-comparison reference, use a light ivory presentation board rather than a dark gallery board. Arrange D1, D2, and D3 in three aligned columns. Within each column, show the true-ratio front above its matching true-ratio back, add restrained paper shadows, and place a compact evaluation strip below. Do not place vertical cards inside horizontal white frames.

In this selected system, the three backs form a controlled family:

- **D1 Photo + Oil Fusion back:** use a source-derived atmosphere color as the dominant field when it strengthens the product; film-white remains valid only when the front genuinely requires restraint. A softly transformed photographic or painterly echo may grow from one lower corner; postal rules and type remain crisp and high-contrast.
- **D2 Artist Travel Archive back:** use a source-derived ground with restrained archival paper character. A subject-derived pencil/ink study or construction sketch sits near the lower or outer edge; short handwritten-style placeholders such as `PLACE`, `TIME`, or `FIELD NOTE` may appear. One piece or a purposeful 2–4-piece tape ensemble may attach and connect the study, label, seam, and postal structure; every piece needs a distinct role.
- **D3 Museum Art Collection back:** use a controlled source color or clean ivory according to the artwork; museum restraint must not become empty beige. Use a small museum accession label or edition block, precise high-contrast hairlines, an address treatment, and a visible stamp position.

Keep the subject echo meaningful: preserve a complete landmark silhouette, a readable landscape horizon, or an intact portrait/context fragment. Do not use an arbitrary crop merely to fill a corner. The ivory comparison board is only presentation staging; it does not force the postcard backs themselves to be ivory. This warm comparison system supersedes the full-art and floating-panel systems only when the user explicitly chooses this reference direction.

### Vertical presentation rule

A vertical postcard is a complete vertical canvas, not a vertical image placed inside a horizontal white card. Show front and back at their native vertical ratio. In proof sheets, place vertical front and back side by side or stack them on a neutral/dark gallery field; never add fake white side borders or wide white padding. Any film-white area visible around a vertical design must belong to the actual designed card, not to the presentation frame.

Choose one system. Systems 1–20 are the preferred open/asymmetrical families; they avoid a mandatory central split.

## Back-side direction matching

The back is mandatory for a postcard unless the user explicitly requests a front-only or art-master-only study. Match it to the selected direction:

- **D1 Photo + Oil Fusion:** use a restrained painted-light or canvas-texture echo from the front, then keep postal functions crisp and photographic. Suitable systems include image echo, floating stamp window, horizontal address shelf, corner correspondence, or full art back with a pale writing veil.
- **D2 Artist Travel Archive:** use neutral archival paper, source-derived contour/sketch evidence, registration marks, and an open asymmetric correspondence field. Suitable systems include field-note margin, edge index, correspondence grid, arch echo, or twin islands.
- **D3 Museum Art Collection:** use matte cotton-rag character, sparse curatorial hierarchy, precise hairlines, and minimal image echo. Suitable systems include museum accession back, offset address island, archive footer, museum caption back, or blank collector back with mailing functions retained when required.

Orientation must match the finished front exactly. A horizontal front receives a horizontal back; a vertical front receives a vertical back. Do not rotate the postal layout independently.

### Functional rules

- Use 0–4 address lines depending on the chosen system; an open address zone may replace printed lines.
- Reserve a clear stamp position, but it may be indicated by a corner mark, perforated frame, small caption, or image window rather than a literal box.
- Use a divider only when it strengthens the composition.
- Prefer layouts 1–20 when the front is already structured, cinematic, or collage-heavy.
- Use at most one organizing motif on the back: line, frame, orbit, image echo, or metadata bar.
- Preserve generous writing space.
- Optional lyric, note, caption, place, date, or credit must be supplied or requested.
- For a non-mailable art card, mailing constraints may be relaxed, but label it clearly as an art back.
- For actual mailing, verify the destination postal service's latest clear-zone and size rules before final production.
